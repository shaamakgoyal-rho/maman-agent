Maman macOS · local-only · developer preview

It notices the work you stopped noticing.

Maman watches only the apps you allow, spots a workflow you keep repeating, and offers a helper — but only after replaying it against your own past runs and showing you the score. Everything stays on your Mac.

Five steps, about two minutes

  1. Download and open the disk image.

    Your browser will save Maman.dmg. Double-click it.

  2. Drag Maman into Applications.

    The window shows both — drag left to right. Then eject the disk image.

  3. Right-click Maman in Applications and choose Open.

    Do not double-click the first time. macOS will warn that the developer cannot be verified; click Open to confirm. This is only needed once, and it is required because this preview is not yet notarized.

  4. Grant Accessibility, then click Always Allow on the keychain prompt.

    Maman opens System Settings for you — flip its switch under Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Without it Maman cannot see the shape of your work and will honestly tell you it is not observing. The keychain prompt is macOS asking permission to unlock the encrypted database it just created for you.

  5. Choose what it may watch.

    Onboarding asks which apps and sites are fair game. Nothing is watched until you say so. When the pet's dot turns green, Maman is running.

How you know it worked: a small bar appears at the bottom of the window you are working in, with a green dot and a line like Watching: Month-end accrual journals — 6 of 8 checks. Green means it is genuinely observing. Any other colour means it is not, and it will say why.

Optional: browser detail

Maman sees native apps through macOS Accessibility. For richer detail inside Chrome — which record you were on, not just that a browser was open — there is a companion extension. It is not required, and Maman works without it.

The extension is not yet on the Chrome Web Store. Until it is, browser workflows are still detected, but their names stay coarser.

What Maman never collects

These are not settings you have to find. They are properties of how it is built.

No keystrokes
It registers no keyboard tap. Ever.
No screen capture
Nothing is captured by default. Teach Mode is the one exception, is off until you enable it, and is described below.
No secure fields
Passwords, payment fields and password managers are refused outright.
No private browsing
Private windows are a hard boundary.

Teach Mode: the one thing that sends pictures of your screen

Everything above is about the shape of your work. Teach Mode is the single exception, and it works differently enough to deserve its own section.

When you want Maman to learn a workflow you can start a Teach Mode session. While it runs, Maman takes pictures of the apps you picked and sends those pictures to Anthropic to work out what you were doing. Nothing else Maman does sends images of your screen anywhere.

  • It is off until you turn it on, and turning it on does not start anything — it only makes starting a session possible.
  • A session lasts at most 15 minutes and stops itself. There is no always-on mode.
  • Only the apps you pick for that session. Anything else on your screen is not captured — not blanked out afterwards, never captured.
  • Before a picture is sent, anything that looks like a password, card number, API key, one-time code or password-manager popup is blacked out. If a password field has focus, or too much of the screen would need blacking out, the whole picture is thrown away instead of sent.
  • Pictures are never saved to disk and never synced. They are read once and dropped.
  • Keystrokes are still never read. That one has no exception, in Teach Mode or anywhere.
  • What Maman thinks it saw can be wrong, unlike the rest of its observation, so it shows you every reading and remembers only the ones you confirm.

What it does record

The shape of your work: which category of app, what kind of element you touched, how long a step took. Text you see is hashed before it is stored, so a label becomes an anonymous fingerprint rather than words.

Recorded Not recorded
"a table was read in a spreadsheet" the contents of the table
"a field was committed on an invoice" what you typed into it
a renewal date read off a record whose renewal it is

Where it lives

In an encrypted database on your Mac, with the key in your login keychain. There is no account and no server — the sync path is off unless you deliberately connect one, and the only thing that ever leaves your Mac on its own is a Teach Mode picture, described above. Privacy → Delete data removes any app's history, or all of it, and writes a tombstone so it cannot come back.

Nothing changes without you

A helper is drafted, replayed against your own past runs, and shown to you with its score before it may touch anything. Actions that alter a real record need your explicit approval, every time, and some are capped so they can only ever draft.

Notice → prove → offer

  1. Notice. Repeated sequences are grouped into a candidate workflow. A one-off is not a pattern.
  2. Prove. A draft helper is replayed against your own recorded runs. The card shows the real score — "tested against your last 21 runs, matched 19". Below a floor of runs no score is claimed at all, because a 2-out-of-2 match is noise dressed as proof.
  3. Offer. Only then does a card appear, named after the work — "Update account records in Salesforce" — with the steps it observed spelled out.

It also knows when to speak

Some work is periodic. Tell Maman when your close starts and it can offer to pre-stage month-end work a few days ahead; renewals surface off dates read from the records themselves. Quiet hours, a daily budget, audit weeks and full-screen presentations all silence it, and a card held during a quiet week is queued rather than dropped.

When it does not know, it says so

If a helper has not been tested enough, the card says there is nothing to claim rather than inventing a number. If a date on a record is ambiguous — is 03/04 March or April? — it stays silent instead of guessing.

"Maman is damaged and can't be opened"

macOS quarantines apps downloaded from the internet, and an unsigned preview sometimes gets this message instead of the milder one. Clear the quarantine flag: 

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Maman.app

Only run this for software you meant to install. It tells macOS you accept responsibility for this app, which is precisely what notarization would otherwise establish on our side.

The dot is not green

That is the point of the dot — it goes green only when Maman is genuinely observing.

  • Grey or amber: observation is paused, or the observer is still starting.
  • "Accessibility permission needed": the switch in System Settings is off. Maman can reopen that pane for you from Settings.
  • "Needs keychain access" (red): macOS did not release the database key. Quit Maman, open it again, and click Always Allow when prompted. Do not delete the keychain item — that orphans the encrypted database.

No suggestions yet

Expected early on. A workflow has to recur enough times, on enough separate days, and then survive a replay test. Maman shows exactly which bar it has yet to clear rather than leaving you guessing.

The status bar is in the way

Drag it anywhere — where you drop it, it stays. It can also dock to the window you are working in, let clicks pass through it, or be hidden entirely.

Removing it completely

rm -rf /Applications/Maman.app
rm -rf ~/Library/Application\ Support/com.maman.desktop

The second line deletes the encrypted database. Privacy → Delete device data does the same from inside the app, including the keychain key.
